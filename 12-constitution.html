About This Document

The Constitution of Mili Mehwar is the foundational legal and philosophical document of the movement. It establishes the organisation's purpose, membership structure, governance bodies, decision-making processes, and the principles that bind all members. The following is a structured summary of its key provisions. To read the full document, please download the PDF above.

I

Chapter One

Name, Identity & Legal Status

  • The organisation is formally named Mili Mehwar — the Afghan National Movement (ملی محور — جنبش ملی افغانستان).
  • Mili Mehwar is a non-partisan, non-profit civil society organisation with no affiliation to any government, political party, or armed faction.
  • The organisation operates internationally, with branches or representative members in any country where a minimum of twenty registered members reside.
  • The official languages of the organisation are Dari, Pashto, and English. All official documents are published in all three languages.
II

Chapter Two

Objectives & Principles

  • To advance a peaceful, Afghan-led political solution to the ongoing crisis in Afghanistan, grounded in democracy, human rights, and the rule of law.
  • To facilitate structured national dialogue among Afghans of all backgrounds, with the aim of developing shared political positions and recommendations.
  • To advocate internationally for Afghan rights, sovereignty, and inclusion — particularly for women, minorities, and civil society — in any political process.
  • To educate and empower Afghan communities through civic education, book clubs, research publications, and cultural programmes.
  • The organisation commits to the principles of non-violence, inclusion, transparency, accountability, and respect for all cultures and ethnicities within Afghanistan.
III

Chapter Three

Membership

  • Membership is open to all Afghans aged 18 and above, regardless of ethnicity, religion, gender, or place of residence.
  • Membership is free of charge. No financial contribution is required to join, vote, or hold a position within the organisation.
  • Members are required to uphold the Constitution, act in accordance with the organisation's principles, and refrain from actions that bring the organisation into disrepute.
  • Membership may be revoked by a two-thirds majority of the Executive Council in cases of serious misconduct, violation of the Constitution, or affiliation with armed groups.
  • Honorary membership may be granted to non-Afghans who have made exceptional contributions to the cause of Afghan rights and sovereignty.
IV

Chapter Four

Governance Structure

  • General Assembly (Loya Jirga): The supreme decision-making body, composed of all registered members. Convenes at minimum once every two years, and has authority to elect or remove leadership, amend the Constitution, and set strategic direction.
  • Executive Council: A body of 11 elected members responsible for implementing the General Assembly's decisions, managing day-to-day operations, and representing the organisation externally.
  • Chairman: Elected by the General Assembly to a two-year term, renewable once. Chairs the Executive Council and represents the movement publicly.
  • Oversight Committee: An independent body of three members elected by the General Assembly, responsible for auditing finances and ensuring Constitutional compliance.
  • Working Groups: Specialised committees on Women's Rights, Education, Economics, International Affairs, and Media, each led by an elected coordinator.
V

Chapter Five

Finances & Resources

  • The organisation is funded through voluntary member donations, grants from civil society foundations, and proceeds from events. No funding is accepted from any government or political party.
  • All financial accounts are audited annually by the Oversight Committee and a summary financial report is published and made available to all members.
  • No member of the Executive Council may receive personal financial benefit from the organisation's funds beyond the reimbursement of documented expenses.
  • In the event of dissolution, all remaining assets shall be transferred to a registered charitable organisation with compatible objectives, as decided by the General Assembly.
VI

Chapter Six

Amendments & Dissolution

  • Amendments to this Constitution require a two-thirds majority vote at a duly convened General Assembly, with at least 30 days' written notice to all members.
  • Chapters relating to the organisation's core objectives and non-partisan status may not be amended under any circumstances.
  • Dissolution of the organisation requires a three-quarters majority at a specially convened General Assembly, attended by no fewer than one hundred registered members.

This is a summary of the Mili Mehwar Constitution for public reference. The full legally binding text is available in the PDF download above. Current version: 2.1 — ratified at the General Assembly, October 2023.
